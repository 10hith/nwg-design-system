Data → Layout

A decision taxonomy for picking the right NWG layout based on the shape of the data you're communicating. Pair this with the layout gallery and the token sheet.

1 · Decision tree

Ask three questions in order. Each answer narrows the layout down to one or two candidates.

Q1

What is the purpose of this slide?

→ Opening / transition / closing
Use a ceremonial layout: 01 Cover, 02 Section Divider, 12 Disclaimer. Skip to rendering.
→ Orienting the audience to agenda / speakers
Use 03 Agenda. Skip to rendering.
→ Carrying an argument with data
Continue to Q2.
Q2

What is the shape of the data?

→ Several parallel takeaways (assertions)
04 Statement Stack
→ Parallel peers with the same attributes (equal weight)
05 3-Column Pillars (3–4 peers) or 06 Comparison Matrix (more attributes)
→ Independent magnitude metrics (KPIs)
07 Stat Grid
→ Trend / change over time + narrative
08 Chart + Narrative
→ Part-to-whole composition of one total
09 Hub & Spoke (donut)
→ Time-ordered sequence / story
10 Process / Journey
→ Hierarchical relationships (parent → children)
11 Hierarchy Tree
Q3

How many items? If the answer from Q2 isn't comfortable at your item count, fall back:

1–2 items
Use 04 Statement Stack or a hero 07 Stat Grid — don't waste a 3-col layout.
3–5 items
Preferred zone for 05 3-Col Pillars, 07 Stat Grid, 10 Process.
6+ items
Switch to 06 Comparison Matrix (tabular) or split across two slides.

2 · Data shapes & their layouts

Each card defines a data shape, its signature, the primary layout and acceptable alternatives, plus common anti-patterns.

Shape A

Parallel assertions

Several short "we are / we will / we have" takeaways that need equal visual weight. No inherent order.
Best layout
04 Statement Stack
Content req.
3–5 rows · each ≤ 18 words · italic bold · check-ring icon · shared subject ("our …", "we …")
Example
Acquisition rationale summary — 5 reasons a deal is strategically attractive.
Avoid when: items have different weights, dependencies, or an underlying sequence — use 10 Process instead. Never exceed 5 rows; split across slides.
Shape B

Equal peers with shared attributes

2–5 items of the same class (pillars, priorities, business units). Each has an icon, a name, and 3–5 supporting bullets.
Best layout
05 3-Column Pillars06 Comparison Matrix (if >2 attributes per peer)
Content req.
3–4 columns · icon + heading + 3–5 bullets each · optional banner across bottom with combined outcome
Example
"Disciplined growth / Simplification / Risk management" strategic pillars.
Avoid when: peers are being compared on many distinct attributes — promote to 06 Matrix. Never mix peer counts (e.g. 3 columns of different widths).
Shape C

Multi-attribute comparison

Same set of attributes measured for 2–4 entities. Often used to compare business units, market segments, or vendors.
Best layout
06 Comparison Matrix
Content req.
Row labels in left gutter (tinted pills) · 3–4 entity columns · cell content is short factual phrases · one column may be emphasised with dark fill
Example
"Leading positions across our three businesses" — Retail / Private / C&I × customers × share × RoE.
Avoid when: cells contain long prose — replace with a 2-up of 08 Chart + Narrative. Also avoid for just 2 entities — use 2-col split.
Shape D

Independent KPIs / magnitudes

3–8 numbers that each say something distinct about the subject, joined only by the slide's thesis. No trend, no hierarchy.
Best layout
07 Stat Grid
Content req.
Big slab-serif number · one-line label · optional "—•— Group title —•—" divider to separate sub-groups · sources as superscripts
Example
"Climate progress highlights": £32.6bn · 0.7% · 330k · 46% — four wins framed as one story.
Avoid when: the numbers share a time dimension — use 08 Chart + Narrative. Avoid mixing units that require footnote-heavy explanation in one row.
Shape E

Trend & supporting context

One chart carrying the quantitative argument plus 2–4 contextual drivers or implications.
Best layout
08 Chart + Narrative
Content req.
Chart on tinted left half (bar/stacked bar/line) · headline row on right with category labels and arrow-bullets · current-year bar in purple.700, prior years in purple.300
Example
"UK wealth market growing ahead of GDP" — stacked bar + 3 demand tailwinds.
Avoid when: you have no narrative (just raw numbers) — use 07 Stat Grid. Avoid more than one chart per slide — split.
Shape F

Part-to-whole composition

Breaking one total (portfolio, revenue, customer base) into 4–8 slices, usually anchored to a single headline number.
Best layout
09 Hub & Spoke (Donut)Map callouts — for geographic splits
Content req.
Central big number · donut palette uses purple.900 → purple.100 in decreasing size order · outer labels or callouts carry 1–2 lines of context per slice
Example
"Credit exposure diversified across sectors" — £74bn loans donut with sector labels.
Avoid when: you have only 2 slices — use a 2-col split. Avoid pies over 6 slices; group smaller slices into "Other".
Shape G

Sequence / time-ordered story

A 3-step journey: dated milestones with a short caption and an outcome row (who did what).
Best layout
10 Process / Journey
Content req.
3 dated cards with short captions · optional image/thumbnail · vertical "NWG actions" label on the left of the outcome row · arrows between steps when causality matters
Example
"Our relationship with Zenobē over 5 years" — three phases and NWG's evolving role.
Avoid when: steps are non-sequential or the dates don't matter — use 05 Pillars. Avoid more than 4 steps on one slide.
Shape H

Hierarchy / taxonomy

A parent entity decomposes into children (segments, products, divisions). Often paired with a flat grid of attributes underneath.
Best layout
11 Hierarchy Tree
Content req.
Parent in a dark purple box · bracket → 3–5 children in mid-purple · optional gold outline to highlight focus child · flat "Products we offer" grid beneath
Example
"Commercial Mid-Market" → 5 size segments (Large SME → Regional Large) + products grid.
Avoid when: hierarchy is more than 3 deep — switch to 06 Matrix or split. Avoid when siblings vary wildly in size; the tree implies parity.
Shape X

Ceremonial / orientation

Slides that carry no data argument: cover, section dividers, agenda, disclaimer. Their job is tone and orientation.
Best layouts
01 Cover02 Section Divider03 Agenda12 Disclaimer
Rule of thumb
Use at the start, between chapters, and at the end. Don't use inside chapters unless genuinely transitioning.
